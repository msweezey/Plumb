# Plumb — shared consumer personas (harness/shared/personas.md)

One persona set for every B2C brand in Plumb. Brands don't own personas; each brand brief names which shared personas are its primary and secondary audience. The shared contact spine (retail-spine-v2, 250 people) tags every contact with one of these persona IDs, so the same people play the same roles in every brand's tests and results compare cleanly across brands and quadrants.

Each field is marked **[E]** when it rests on published research (sources at the end) or **[H]** when it is a Plumb working assumption to be checked with the vertical boards.

## GZ — Gen Z (18–29)
- **Life stages:** students, first jobs, first apartments; tight or irregular budgets. [H]
- **Discovery:** friends and family recommendations and in-store browsing come first; heavy use of Instagram and TikTok; more likely than older adults to research brands on forums. [E]
- **Social:** about eight in ten adults 18–29 use Instagram. [E]
- **Contact preference:** email is the top choice for contacting a business. [E]
- **Social commerce:** discovers and buys on social, but some abandon over trust. [E]
- **What convinces:** real people and community proof, values that match their own, price stated plainly. [E/H]
- **Turn-offs:** brands trying too hard with slang, fake scarcity, undisclosed paid creators. [H]
- **Voice that lands:** short, direct, a little playful; one idea per line. [H]
- **Offer sensitivity:** high; responds to clear price points and bundles. [H]

## MIL — Millennial (30–45)
- **Life stages:** established careers, young kids or none, first homes. [H]
- **Discovery:** the most active social shoppers of the four: highest share discovering, researching and buying through social. [E]
- **Device:** mobile-first for research, browsing and buying. [E]
- **Timing:** more likely than older shoppers to start holiday shopping early. [E]
- **What convinces:** practical benefit plus a values reason; reviews; versatility (price per wear, multi-use). [H]
- **Turn-offs:** guilt-driven messaging, vague claims, clutter. [H]
- **Voice that lands:** warm, efficient, lightly witty. [H]
- **Offer sensitivity:** medium-high; values bundles and loyalty credit. [H]

## GX — Gen X (46–61)
- **Life stages:** peak earning years, teens or adult children, caring for parents. [H]
- **Discovery:** uses social for discovery but researches and buys there less than Millennials; rarely abandons over trust once engaged. [E]
- **What convinces:** transparency, product performance and reviews rather than flashy advertising; skeptical but loyal once won. [E]
- **Turn-offs:** hype, gimmicks, being talked down to. [H]
- **Voice that lands:** plain, specific, confident; facts first. [H]
- **Offer sensitivity:** medium; quality and durability matter more than discount depth. [H]

## BB — Boomer (62–80)
- **Life stages:** retired or winding down, grandchildren, downsizing. [H]
- **Social:** YouTube and Facebook are the only platforms used by a majority in every age group; Instagram use drops to about one in five at 65+. [E]
- **Contact preference:** email, web chat and phone. [E]
- **Social commerce:** least likely to discover on social and most likely to abandon over trust. [E]
- **What convinces:** value, service and trust: clear returns, guarantees, phone support. [E]
- **Turn-offs:** slang, tiny text, unclear checkout and return terms. [H]
- **Voice that lands:** courteous, complete sentences, no jargon. [H]
- **Accessibility:** favor larger type and high contrast; never rely on emoji for meaning. [H]

## How brands use the set
- Each brand brief lists a **primary** and **secondary** persona (e.g. Pact: MIL primary, GX secondary).
- Tasks name the persona they target (e.g. E1 writes one email for MIL, one for GX).
- Scoring rule MSG-15 (message fits persona) judges against this file for every brand.
- B2B verticals (tech/SaaS) need a separate shared set built on buying-group roles; not covered here.
