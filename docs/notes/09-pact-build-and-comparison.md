# Pact — harness build and method comparison

Pilot for the retail public build, 22 Nov 2026. Two methods were run on Pact, then compared:
- **Method A (assume, then test):** 34 assumptions written from prior knowledge before any source was opened, then scored against evidence.
- **Method B (reverse-engineer):** collect public material first, then derive the brand book bottom-up.

Evidence pulled: wearpact.com (home, about, sustainability, standards, shipping, a category page, a product page with reviews), 29 live Meta ads from 1,257 in the library, 12 recent Instagram posts (own and tagged), ~150 Google Shopping listings, brand colors and logos from Brand.dev, and press and company data. Data cost: under $0.10.

## Headline findings

1. **The brand has moved, and memory hasn't.** Pact now leads with personal wellness ("clothes have ingredients", made without harsh chemicals, toxic dyes and PFAS, "Dress Yourself Well"). Method A assumed the older planet-and-ethics brand ("Earth's Favorite"). 5 of 34 assumptions were wrong or outdated, and those are the beliefs a model is most likely to carry. They became six memory traps in the harness.
2. **Public material yields voice, claims and offers, not rules.** Of 181 rules, 62 are observed, 15 inferred and 104 set by Plumb (118 brand-layer, 63 compliance-layer). Specs, legal overlays, process and most visual rules aren't visible from outside, so a public build is roughly one third evidence and two thirds harness. That supports swapping in partner guidelines later.
3. **Real brands come with real inconsistencies, which make strong traps.** Free shipping is $99+ in the policy but $100+ in metadata. The water claim appears as 81% and, in older copy, 95%. The Standards page says every garment is certified organic, but socks contain 20% recycled nylon. "MSRP" reference prices sit on nearly every product during a sitewide sale. Each became a conflict or a critical rule.
4. **Neither method is enough alone.** A pointed the search at the right places (claims, pricing, legal exposure). B found the repositioning and nine things A had no hypothesis for. A also covered things B can't see (order value, email cadence), but only as guesses.

## Method B: the reverse-engineered brand book (summary)

- **Essence:** getting dressed as self-care; certified organic essentials, gentle on skin, priced for everyday life. Tagline "Dress Yourself Well".
- **Voice:** warm, clear, grounded, easygoing. On site and email, plain and calm with no emoji. In ads, Title Case headlines that lead on price, with the CTA always "Shop now". On social, playful, emoji-heavy, comment-to-get-link posts and light slang. Signature phrases: "made with / made without", "not all organic is created equal", "clothes have ingredients".
- **Messaging pillars:** gentle on skin (no harsh chemicals, toxic dyes or PFAS); verified standards (GOTS, Fair Trade, offsets); organic within reach (prices from $10).
- **Product page pattern:** hook line, what it is, how it fits, how to wear it; a "Wellness ingredients" module (MADE WITH / MADE WITHOUT / CERTIFIED); a blend footnote; reviews labeled as incentivized.
- **Visual cues (from site CSS):** black wordmark; Warm Charcoal #4D4A43 text and buttons; White, Off-White #F7F7F5 and Stone #EBEAE8 surfaces; Sale Red #C8422D prices; Wine #7D2D3B sale and gift links; Earth Green #38761D sustainability headings; Tan #A98D6D loyalty. Type: Juana serif headings, Averta sans body and UI, GT Eesti product titles and prices. Lowercase hero lines. The site's struck-through regular price (#94928E) fails AA contrast on white.
- **Commerce:** permanent reference "MSRP" with everyday prices below it, frequent sitewide events, bundles and sets, loyalty credit, free shipping at $99+, 30-day returns, clearance final sale.
- **Channels:** heavy Meta (including catalog ads with a live price slot), Instagram creators and giveaways, wellness events, retail partners.
- **Legal exposure seen:** reference pricing, absolute organic claims, offset wording, health-adjacent "toxic" framing, creator posts without visible disclosure, inconsistent shipping threshold.

## What this means for the other three brands

- Keep both methods, in this order: write assumptions first (15 minutes), collect evidence, test, then reverse-engineer. The wrong assumptions are the cheapest source of memory traps.
- Expect about 30% observed rules per brand. Tag provenance on every rule so partner kits can replace harness rules cleanly.
- Mine each brand's own inconsistencies for conflicts; they are more realistic than invented ones.
- Budget about $0.10–0.20 of data per brand, plus fonts and imagery checks this pilot skipped.
