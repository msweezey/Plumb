# Plumb: business idea

> **Know what good looks like.**
> Proof, not promises. Scores can't be bought.

Plumb is the standard for AI-native marketing work: **AI marketing evals for AI martech.**

Source of truth: Sweezey's brief of 21 Nov 2026. Anything marked *Proposal* below is a suggestion that fills a gap in the brief and is open for review. Prices are `[Price]` until tested.

---

## 1. The one-line idea

We point AI agents at marketing platforms through their MCP servers, make them do real marketing-ops work, score every run, have working practitioners grade the outcome, and publish the results. The public scores are the Plumb Index. The playbook behind every score is a forkable Plumb Line.

## 2. The problem

Marketing teams are wiring agents into their stacks. Vendors say their tools are "agent-ready." Nobody measures it.

- Buyers can't tell which platforms an agent can actually operate, how often it needs a human, what it costs per task, or whether it breaks things when it writes.
- Vendors can't prove their MCP server works in a way an enterprise buyer will trust.
- Marketing leaders have no reference for what an AI-native team, stack or workflow should look like, or what numbers to hold it to.
- AI labs need realistic, expert-graded marketing tasks and environments to train and evaluate on, and they don't have them.

## 3. Why now

Major martech vendors now ship official MCP servers (HubSpot, Salesforce Marketing Cloud, Adobe Marketo, Klaviyo, Canva and others). The buyer's question has shifted from **"which tool has the best UI?"** to **"which tool can my agents operate?"** There is no independent answer to the second question yet.

## 4. What we do

### Three levels of autonomy

Every task can be run at three levels, so buyers see how far each platform lets them hand over.

| Level | Setup | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Human in the loop** | A marketer uses the SaaS tool's own UI and built-in AI | How much the AI features help a skilled operator |
| **Human on the loop** | A marketer works from an IDE or assistant (Claude, Antigravity and similar) through MCP connections | Tool coverage, accuracy, how often the human steps in |
| **Human out of the loop** | Agents are given the task and run it on their own | End-to-end success, cost, and whether they can write safely |

### Disciplines

Strategy, media, content, social, creator and more, across B2B and B2C, enterprise down.

### How an eval works

1. **Task library.** Real marketing-ops jobs, for example: build a 3-email nurture; pull last month's results and pause the losers; clone and localize a live campaign; route a lead form; clean 500 contacts. Covers B2B and B2C workflows from enterprise down. Boards choose the tasks.
2. **Harness.** Several AI models × each platform's MCP server × a sandbox account. Same tasks, same models, every platform.
3. **Scoring.** Every run is scored on:
   - task success
   - accuracy
   - tool calls / steps
   - cost per task
   - time
   - human interventions
   - read vs. write coverage
   - safety (can it write without breaking things)
   - final outcome, graded by practitioners in that discipline
4. **Output.** Each eval produces a full eval set covering every tool combination, with time, cost, review rounds and team broken down by each step of the process. The headline goes on the Index leaderboard. Behind each result sits a full, forkable playbook (a Plumb Line) so anyone can adopt the play.

### Focus areas and boards

Evals are organized by focus area: **Strategy, Email, Creative & content, Ads & media**, across B2B and B2C and by major vertical (retail, B2B and others).

Each focus area has a **board of practitioners**. Board members:

- choose the tasks
- review results before anything is published
- add the "board take" to each result
- publish content we prepare under their own names

The boards guide the research and are our marketing arm. Their credibility is the product's credibility.

### Knowledge of AI-native marketing

Every eval also teaches us how AI-native marketing work is actually done: stacks, playbooks, team structure, roles and responsibilities, and job descriptions. We package that so teams can build it.

## 5. Who buys (in priority order)

| # | Buyer | What they need | What they buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Martech and AI tool vendors** moving upmarket | Independent proof their MCP server works, to win enterprise deals | Certification ("Plumbed"), full results, fix lists |
| 2 | **Enterprise marketing teams** | AI-native playbooks, stack information and benchmarks to target. Replaces traditional analyst services | Enterprise: playbooks, teams, roles, JDs, stack and vendor analysis, benchmarks |
| 3 | **AI labs** | Realistic task suites and environments for training and evals | Licensed task suites, rubrics and sandbox environments, directly or through partners such as Mercor or Surge |
| 4 | **Agencies and integrators** | A neutral basis for stack recommendations | Benchmarks and certification |

**Not a fit:** small brands without ops teams; pre-revenue tools.

## 7. How the pieces reinforce each other

One eval engine produces everything:

1. Boards pick tasks → we build sandboxes and rubrics.
2. Runs produce scores → **Index** (free, public) and **monthly report**.
3. Winning runs become **Plumb Lines** → the community forks and grades them, which improves the tasks.
4. Results and Plumb Lines roll up into the **Enterprise** kit (stack, team, roles, JDs, benchmarks).
5. Vendors who score below the standard buy **certification** testing and fix lists to get there.
6. The same expert-graded suites and sandboxes are licensed to **AI labs** behind the scenes.

Public results are what make the paid products credible. Labs licensing is what funds depth in the task library.

## 8. Integrity rules

"Scores can't be bought" has to be true in the mechanics, not just the copy. *Proposal:*

- The public Index runs on every platform in a category whether or not the vendor pays.
- Vendors pay for testing, full results and fix lists. They never pay for a score, a rank or a board take.
- "Plumbed" is awarded only when a platform meets a published standard set by the board. No vendor sits on a board.
- Every score shows its method and sample beside it: models, runs, tasks, date.
- Results are reviewed by the board before publishing. Vendors see their own results before publication for factual corrections only.
- Labs licensing never includes vendor-confidential results.

## 9. Go to market

*Proposal*, built from the brief:

- **Boards first.** Recruit one board per focus area. Their names, posts and board takes are the launch.
- **Index as the hook.** Launch with one focus area (Email is the most MCP-ready) and a small set of platforms, published free.
- **Monthly report** as the recurring reason to come back and the first paid touch.
- **Vendor motion.** Every platform on the Index gets its results; those below the standard are offered certification testing and fix lists.
- **Enterprise motion.** Lead with benchmarks for the buyer's vertical, then the playbooks, team design and JDs.
- **Labs motion.** Package the task suites and sandboxes once two focus areas are live; sell directly and through data partners.

## 12. Out of scope

Plumb is **not** a brand-ranking index, an agentic commerce index, a paid/owned/earned tracker or a CMO strategy readout. Those were earlier directions and are retired. Agentic commerce is context only.
